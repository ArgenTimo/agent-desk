# 06 · The two numbers this project set out to be judged by

[`docs/09-roadmap.md`](../09-roadmap.md) ends with a section called **What is measured**, and it is
unambiguous about why those two and not others:

> Two numbers, from the beginning, because they are the ones that say whether this worked:
>
> - **Terminal opens to check status**, per day. Phase 1 exists to drive this to zero.
> - **Ideas captured, and of those, promoted.** Capture with no promotion means the inbox is a
>   drain, not a notebook.

**Neither is anywhere in the product.** Not on the board, not in the pool, not behind a link, not
in a log line. Searched: nothing renders either, and nothing records the first one at all.

Counted out of the author's own store, which is the point:

| | |
|---|---|
| ideas captured | **479** |
| of those, built | **467** |
| discarded | 10 |
| still open | 2 |
| filed out to a tracker | 256 |
| captured in the last seven days | 478 |

That is the tool working — an inbox that is emphatically not a drain — and there is no surface on
which anybody can see it. A console that shows what today's questions cost (`$0.55 today`, and it
argues in its own comments that a number behind a link is a number nobody is shown) says nothing
at all about whether the thing it exists for is happening.

## Who this is

The person deciding whether to keep using this, or whether the hour they spent on it last Tuesday
paid for itself. Also the person who wrote that roadmap section and has never once been able to
read those numbers.

---

## 1 · The pool says whether it is a notebook or a drain

> As someone looking at the idea column, I want to see how many thoughts went in and how many came
> out the other side, so I can tell whether writing things down here is worth doing.

**Done when** the pool's own column carries the two counts, in its own words, without a page of its
own — a number behind a link is a number nobody is shown.

## 2 · Terminal opens are counted honestly or not claimed

> As someone who wants to know whether this console has replaced opening a terminal, I want the
> number it shows to be a number it can actually stand behind.

The console cannot see a terminal somebody opened themselves. It *can* see every one it opened —
`opening.open_it` is the `go to it` button, and every press is this console handing somebody back
to a terminal because the board did not answer their question.

That is a different number from the roadmap's, and it is the honest half of it: not "terminal
opens per day" but "times the board sent you to a terminal". [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md) rule
five is exactly about not letting the first pass for the second.

**Done when** the presses this console makes are recorded and shown, said as what they are, and
the roadmap says which half of its own measure is being answered.

## 3 · A number that is fine says nothing

> As someone reading the board all day, I want these counts to stay out of my way when there is
> nothing to think about.

The spending counter already makes this argument in its own comment: *a counter that reads "$0.14
of $25.00" all day teaches people to stop reading it.*

**Done when** an inbox that is being emptied is quiet, and one that is filling up without being
emptied says so.

## 4 · The measure is dated, because a week is the unit

> As someone who captured four hundred ideas over months, I want to know about the last week, not
> about all time — an all-time total is a number that stops moving.

**Done when** the count is over a window, and the window is named on the thing itself.

---

## What was deliberately not asked for

**A dashboard.** A page of graphs is a page nobody opens, and this project has said so twice
already — about the spend counter and about the shared view.

**Guessing the roadmap's first number.** Total terminal opens per day is not knowable from here and
an estimate of it would be a guessed status wearing a number.

**Judging the ratio.** The console can say four hundred and sixty-seven of four hundred and
seventy-nine were built. Whether that is good is the reader's, and a console that graded it would
be a console with an opinion about how somebody works.
